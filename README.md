# Contrasting Larval Phenotypes in the Reef-Building Coral Stylophora pistillata Suggest a Diversified Bet-Hedging Strategy

Authors: Liel Uziahu, Elizaveta Skalon, Miri Murgulis, Noam Gazit, Zoe Diemers, Maya Ben-David, Amit Zeltzer, and Tali Mass.

Environmental variability may favor the production of offspring with contrasting phenotypes, yet the contribution of such parental strategies to coral persistence under climate change remains poorly understood. We examined larval variation in the reef-building coral Stylophora pistillata by comparing planulae at the high- and low-fluorescence extremes of the phenotypic range released concurrently by individual colonies. High-Fluorescence and Non-Fluorescence planulae harbored similar Symbiodiniaceae communities dominated by Symbiodinium microadriaticum but differed in morphology, physiology, and gene expression. HF planulae were larger, exhibited elevated green fluorescent protein intensity, and had lower baseline respiration, consistent with greater investment in early settlement and growth. In contrast, NF planulae were smaller, lacked detectable GFP, had a thicker ectoderm and elevated lipid utilization, suggesting increased investment in dispersal and stress preparedness. Transcriptomic analyses further distinguished the two morphotypes: HF larvae upregulated genes associated with biomineralization and GFP-like chromoprotein, whereas NF larvae showed enrichment of pathways associated with environmental sensing and cellular stress regulation, including TRP channels and ATF6/FOXO signaling. We then compared settlement and early post-settlement physiology in separate temperature, pH, and light experiments. HF larvae settled at a higher rate than NF larvae in most conditions, including ambient, although no individual treatment comparison was significant; the phenotype effect became resolvable only when treatments were pooled. Post-settlement respiration was the only response showing a condition-dependent phenotype difference, localized to the pH axis, where the direction reversed under moderate acidification; differences under high temperature and low light appeared only in the uncorrected per-treatment comparisons. Among photophysiological parameters, the functional absorption cross-section of PSII (σPSII) and maximum fluorescence yield (Fm) were consistently higher in HF-derived polyps, whereas maximum quantum yield (Fv/Fm) did not differ between phenotypes. Symbiont density remained similar across phenotypes, suggesting that the observed physiological variation was primarily associated with host traits or parental provisioning rather than with differences in symbiont abundance or composition. These findings show that individual S. pistillata colonies release larvae with contrasting traits that persist through early settlement. This intracolonial variation may provide the phenotypic basis for diversified bet-hedging, although its fitness consequences across variable environments and later life stages remain to be determined.
